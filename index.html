01 / Government filings / Payroll

A government system that goes down. Filings that can’t fail.

Picture 05:45. A worker calls in sick. The shift starts at 06:00. A colleague can cover, but the registration on file names the worker who just called. The manager has fifteen minutes to get the replacement declared before work starts.

Read the full case study ↗
Before
  • The founder’s first version ran on Cloud Functions and Firestore.
  • Failed Dimona filings were rerun by hand.
  • About 10,000 shifts, and the system was already struggling to scale.
After
  • No lost filings, no duplicates, no manual reruns.
  • Every filing ends accepted, retried or with a person.
  • 125,000+ shifts ran on the backend I built most of. The platform grew to 1,000+ companies.
  1. 01Three exits

    Every filing ends accepted, retried or with a person. None is silently lost.

  2. 02Safe to repeat

    A filing sent twice is recognised. The government never gets a duplicate.

  3. 03Ask before you send

    After a crash, the system first asks if the filing arrived. Then it decides.

  4. 04Pay that recalculates

    Pay rules are stored with their dates. Old payslips always give the same amount.

Break the filing queue

Shift changes create filings. Three workers take them from the queue and send them to Dimona. Take the government API down, kill a worker mid-filing or flood the queue at 06:00, and watch where every filing ends up.

      02 / Durable agent runtime

      AI agents that finish the job, even after a crash.

      Picture Friday afternoon. Timesheets are missing again. Someone has to email each person, then chase them again on Monday. I built the system that now does this with AI agents, for 300+ employees.

      Read the full case study ↗
      Before
      • Onboarding, work-from-home compliance checks and timesheet chasing were done by hand, over email.
      • Processes took days.
      • Failures were frequent.
      After
      • 20+ live workflows on the runtime I built, serving 300+ employees.
      • Processes that took days finish within a day.
      • Failures went from frequent to near zero.
      1. 01Resume, don’t restart

        A worker crashes at step 4 of 7. Another picks up at step 4. Nothing runs twice.

      2. 02Replay any failure

        Every model answer is recorded. A failed run can be replayed exactly, step by step.

      3. 03Context, not history

        Costs spiked when each step got the full history. Now each step gets only what it needs.

      4. 04Guardrails as layers

        Permissions, limits, checks, human approval and an audit log. A prompt asks; a guardrail enforces.

      03 HOW I THINK

      Beyond the
      happy path.

      Good engineering shows up when things go wrong.

      I care about the gaps between systems. That is where most failures happen.

      A government system times out. A worker crashes halfway through a task. A pay rule changes mid-month. I design for these cases first. Retries never create duplicates. Workflows resume where they stopped. Limits keep a model from going too far. A prompt asks; a guardrail enforces.
